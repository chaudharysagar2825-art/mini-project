# Problem understanding

## SIH problem statement

- **Problem Statement ID:** SIH26094
- **Statement:** AI-Powered Dynamic Mental Health Monitoring and Distress Prediction System for Victims of Atrocities
- **Organization:** Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment
- **Category / theme:** Software / MedTech, BioTech and HealthTech

Manas Setu is a proposed decision-support and referral architecture for this problem. This repository records a future design; it does not provide a working monitoring service or clinical intervention.

## Context and needs

People affected by atrocities may face barriers to seeking support, maintaining safe communication, and describing changing needs over time. The circumstances and preferred ways of receiving support differ by person. Any future system must be trauma-informed, optional, accessible, privacy-preserving, and designed with appropriate domain and lived-experience input.

Possible challenges include limited access to trusted support, inconsistent opportunities to check in, concerns about confidentiality or safety, language and accessibility barriers, and fragmented referral pathways. These are design considerations, not claims about every person's experience.

## Limitations to address

An occasional contact or a single self-report may not capture a person's changing needs. Digital tools can also exclude people, create privacy risks, or misinterpret a response. Automated signals may be incomplete or wrong and must not be treated as a diagnosis or a final clinical, legal, or emergency decision.

## Why ongoing, consent-based check-ins are proposed

With informed, revocable consent, a future service could support a person in recording check-ins over time and make relevant information available to an authorized human reviewer. Participation, channel, cadence, and data sharing would need to be controlled by the person and safe in their circumstances. Monitoring must never be covert or represented as continuous emergency coverage.

## Proposed role of Manas Setu

The proposed system would organize consent, safe-contact preferences, check-ins, explainable decision-support signals, human review, and referral coordination behind clear access boundaries. **AI assists. Humans review. Authorized services respond.** The design does not make autonomous mental-health decisions and does not promise that a particular outcome will follow.
